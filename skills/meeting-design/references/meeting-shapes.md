# Meeting Shapes

Agenda skeletons by what the meeting is for. Adjust the minutes, keep the sequence.

---

## Decide (60 minutes)

| Time | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-5 | Frame | The decision sentence, the decision rule, the time box |
| 5-15 | Silent read | The pre-read, in the room. Questions collected in writing |
| 15-25 | Clarify | Questions of fact only. No positions yet |
| 25-45 | Positions and discussion | Round-robin first so the room hears everyone before it hears anyone twice |
| 45-55 | **The decision** | Protect this block. It is why the meeting exists |
| 55-60 | Close | Decision, owner, date, who gets told |

If you overrun, cut Clarify, not the decision.

---

## Generate (60 to 90 minutes)

| Time | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-10 | Frame the question | Check it does not contain its own answer |
| 10-15 | Agree the criteria | Before any option exists, so they cannot be bent to fit one |
| 15-25 | Silent generation | Everyone writes alone. No talking |
| 25-45 | Round-robin and build | One each, no evaluation, then a second pass building on what is up |
| 45-65 | Cluster and evaluate | Now judgement is allowed, against the criteria from earlier |
| 65-75 | Narrow | To a shortlist, not to a winner. Deciding is a different meeting |
| 75-90 | Close | What happens to the shortlist, who owns it, by when |

Never evaluate before minute 45. The first critical comment ends generation regardless of what the agenda says.

---

## Align (30 to 45 minutes)

| Time | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-5 | Why you are hearing this | Context before content |
| 5-15 | The thing itself | Short. The pre-read carries the detail |
| 15-30 | Questions, and the check | Ask two people to say it back in their own words. This is the whole meeting |
| 30-35 | What changes for each person | Specific to their team, or they will not act on it |

The say-it-back check is what separates alignment from broadcast. If you skip it you have sent an email out loud.

---

## Recurring meetings

A recurring meeting needs a decision sentence like any other, and it usually does not have one. Three fixes:

**Kill the round-robin.** Status goes in writing before the meeting. The meeting time goes to the things that are stuck.

**Run it off a live list.** Open items, owners, dates, on screen. If nothing on the list moved and nothing new is stuck, end early. Ending early is the reward that keeps the format honest.

**Sunset it on a date.** Put a review date on every recurring hold. Meetings almost never get cancelled because nobody owns cancelling them, and a pre-agreed review date fixes that without anyone having to be the person who suggested it.

## Workshops and offsites

Longer sessions fail differently. Three things matter more than the agenda:

- **One question per session block**, not a theme. "What do we stop doing" beats "Strategy"
- **Decide what leaves the room** before it starts. A day that produces a good conversation and no artefact is a day spent
- **Protect the last ninety minutes** for converting output into owners and dates. This block gets eaten by overrunning discussion in almost every offsite that disappoints
