# Meeting Types

Playbooks for the meetings people run most often that are not obviously decide, generate or align. Each one gives the decision-sentence equivalent, the room, a skeleton and the way it usually fails. Adjust the minutes, keep the sequence.

Use `meeting-shapes.md` for decide, generate, align and workshops. Use `decision-rules.md` when the meeting contains a real decision.

---

## 1:1

**What it is for:** the report's agenda, not the manager's. Andy Grove's *High Output Management* argues a 1:1 is the subordinate's meeting; the manager's own status questions belong in writing.

**The sentence:** "By the end, [report] has raised what they most needed to raise, and we both know what each of us does next."

**Room:** two people. Never add a third.

| Time (30 min) | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-15 | Their topics | They set the agenda in a shared doc beforehand. Ask "what else" once before moving on |
| 15-25 | Yours | Feedback, context, anything you need from them. One or two items, not a list |
| 25-30 | Close | Actions with owner and date, written into the shared doc while both are present |

**Fails by:** turning into a status report the manager could have read, or being the first thing cancelled when the week gets busy, which teaches the report their topics rank last. Cancel with a new time, never without one.

**Cancel and replace when:** the shared doc is empty for three weeks running. Ask whether the cadence should drop, rather than filling the time.

## Standup or status meeting

**What it is for:** finding what is stuck. Status itself belongs in writing.

**The sentence:** "By the end, every blocked item has an owner and a next step."

**Room:** the people who can unblock each other. If most attendees only listen, they are informed, not contributors.

| Time (15 min, or 25 for a weekly) | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Before | Written update | Each person adds progress, plans and blockers to a shared doc or thread before the meeting |
| 0-2 | Read the blockers | Out loud, from the doc. No round-robin of progress |
| 2-12 | Work the blockers | Only the people involved. Others may leave once their item is done |
| 12-15 | Close | Owner and date per blocker |

**Fails by:** a round-robin where each person speaks to the leader and nobody to each other. If there are no blockers, end the meeting early and say so; that is the reward that keeps the format honest.

**Cancel and replace when:** the written update is complete and nobody has a blocker. A weekly with no blockers for a month is a doc.

## Customer call

**What it is for:** the customer leaving with a decision, a commitment or a clearer picture of what happens next. This covers discovery calls, check-ins and quarterly reviews.

**The sentence:** "By the end, [customer contact] has agreed to or decided [X], and we both know the next step and its date."

**Room:** your side only is yours to cut. Keep it to the people the customer needs: the owner of the relationship, the person who can answer their hardest question, and a note-taker if you use one. A wall of your team on a call makes the customer feel outnumbered.

| Time (45-60 min) | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-5 | Frame | The purpose, the time, and what you want to leave with. Ask if they want to change it |
| 5-15 | Their priorities | Ask before you present. What matters to them this period |
| 15-40 | The substance | Results or proposal, tied to what they just said matters |
| 40-55 | The decision or ask | One ask, stated plainly |
| 55-60 | Close | Their next step, your next step, dates. Say you will send the written recap |

**Fails by:** a presentation of your own results with no ask, so the customer nods and nothing changes. Send the pre-read a day before and open with their priorities.

**Rule to state:** usually Consult or Delegate toward the customer's sponsor: "You tell us the priorities, we commit to what we can deliver."

## Retro

**What it is for:** changing how the team works, with a small number of actions that get done. The five-phase shape (set the stage, gather data, generate insights, decide what to do, close) is from Esther Derby and Diana Larsen's *Agile Retrospectives*, building on Norm Kerth's *Project Retrospectives*.

**The sentence:** "By the end, we will have chosen one to three changes to try, each with an owner and a date to check it."

**Room:** the team that did the work. Managers join only if the team asks them to, because their presence changes what gets said.

| Time (60 min) | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-5 | Set the stage | Purpose, ground rules, and the previous retro's actions: done or not |
| 5-20 | Gather data | Silent writing first. What happened, not yet what it means |
| 20-35 | Generate insights | Cluster and ask why. Look for causes, not blame |
| 35-50 | **Decide** | Narrow to one to three changes. Test each with consent: any reasoned objection? |
| 50-60 | Close | Owner and check date for each. Record the actions where the team will see them |

**Fails by:** a list of ten complaints and no owners, and by never reviewing the last retro's actions, which teaches the team that actions are decoration. Always open with last time's actions.

## Interview

Two meetings: the interview, and the debrief. Most of the value is in how the debrief is designed.

**The sentence for the debrief:** "By the end, we will have decided whether to make an offer, against criteria agreed before we met the candidate."

**Room:** each interviewer covers a different area, listed in advance. Two interviewers asking the same questions is wasted candidate time. The debrief is the interviewers and the hiring manager as decider.

| Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- |
| Before | Write the criteria and what a strong answer looks like. Assign each interviewer one area |
| During | Each interviewer takes notes against the criteria |
| Before the debrief | Every interviewer submits a written rating and evidence **before** hearing anyone else. This is the same mechanism as silent writing: the first voice sets the frame for everyone after |
| Debrief (30 min) | Read the written feedback silently, then discuss disagreements only. The hiring manager decides, using Consult, and records dissent |

**Fails by:** a debrief where the most senior person speaks first and everyone converges on them, and by criteria invented after meeting the candidate, which get chosen to fit the person already liked.

**Note:** hiring decisions can have legal and fairness rules in your jurisdiction. Defer to your HR or legal guidance on what may be asked and recorded.

## Board or executive review

**What it is for:** a decision or an informed challenge from people with limited time and a lot of context elsewhere.

**The sentence:** "By the end, the board has [approved / challenged / noted] [X], and any condition is written down."

**Room:** the members who vote or hold the relevant accountability, plus the presenter and one person who can answer detail. Everyone else is informed.

| Time (60 min) | Block | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Before | Memo | A short written narrative and the ask in the first paragraph. Send 48 hours ahead where the culture allows, and speak to the biggest sceptic beforehand so nothing in the room is a surprise |
| 0-10 | Silent read | Even if sent ahead. This is the Amazon narrative-memo practice already credited in the README |
| 10-20 | Questions of fact | Presenter answers. No positions yet |
| 20-50 | Challenge and discussion | The memo's riskiest assumption first |
| 50-60 | **Decision and conditions** | State the decision, the vote or rule, and any conditions. Record dissent |

**Fails by:** a presentation that consumes the time and leaves the decision to the last two minutes, and by the ask being buried on page nine. Put the ask first.

**Rule to state:** usually Consent or a vote, set by the governance documents. Check what the charter says before writing the rule into the invite.
